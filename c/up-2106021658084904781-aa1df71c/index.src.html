Recommended

Build the series as code templates (HyperFrames), voiced by the ElevenLabs API on one locked voice and mixed with ffmpeg, run by one small script. Paste a script, get the MP4 and captions about 10 minutes later, for roughly $0.62 a video at 10 a month and $1.43 at 100.

Why code: only the script changes, so code keeps every episode identical, cuts to the word timestamps, renders free and is open source. Runner-up: Creatomate, if nobody will own code. Remotion is similar but paid from 4 people ($25 a seat a month).

Pitfalls, and the fix for each

  • Voice drift (same voice, different read). Model, voice ID and voice settings live in one config file, never the web app. Send a fixed seed; redo single sentences with their neighbours attached.
  • Names said wrong (Maisonneuve, Hochelaga). A pronunciation dictionary on every request (spelling aliases on all models, IPA on Eleven v3/v4). The split step lists unusual names to check first.
  • Music fights the voice. Sidechain ducking in ffmpeg drops the bed ~10 dB under speech (20 ms attack, ~400 ms release); the mix is then normalised to −14 LUFS, the usual online loudness.
  • Every episode looks the same. 6–8 scene templates plus rules in the split prompt: no type twice in a row, a year gets a date card, a person a name strap. One accent and image rule per genre.
  • Captions drift or misspell. Captions come from the voice's character timestamps, so “1535” shows as written, timed as spoken. Max two lines of ~32 characters.
  • Render time, measured. Our 8-second test rendered in 39–50 s on a laptop: plan 7–9 min per episode, about 13 server-hours a month at 100.

Beyond the MVP: what breaks first

  • Throughput breaks first. Queue scripts with status, retries and alerts; add render workers or cloud rendering.
  • Template library. Versioned templates per series; brand colours and fonts in one file.
  • QA step. A review page: frame sheet, loudness, flagged names, approve.
  • Licensing. A log per video for music, images and voice; check the track covers every platform.
  • New formats. 9:16 from the same scenes.json; a new language is a new voice and dictionary.
