Taking over the dispatch platform that moves 2,300 loads a day.

A European logistics group ran its depots on an eleven-year-old system that only one developer still understood. We took it over, made it dependable, migrated it to modern infrastructure without a day of downtime, and now ship to it twice a week.

The situation

The platform planned every load, assigned every driver and recorded every vehicle check across fourteen depots. It worked, mostly. But releases happened once a month on a Sunday night, pages took seconds to load at the morning peak, and the original agency had stopped answering.

The group's operations director needed a team who could take responsibility for a system the business could not switch off for even an hour — and who would explain what they were doing in terms a logistics business understands.

What we inherited

  • 310,000 lines of PHP with no automated tests and no staging environment.
  • One shared database server holding eleven years of loads, checks and audit records.
  • Deployments by copying files over FTP, done by hand by the last remaining developer.
  • A driver app that had not been updated since Android 9.

What we did, year by year

  1. 2020

    Audit and stabilise

    Two-week technical audit, then monitoring, nightly backups, a staging copy of production and a CI/CD pipeline. No features until the system was safe to change.

  2. 2021

    Dispatch board rebuilt

    Planners' main screen rebuilt in React on a new API, released depot by depot behind a switch so each team could fall back for a week.

  3. 2022

    Zero-downtime migration

    Database moved to managed PostgreSQL on AWS with live replication. The cut-over took eleven minutes on a Tuesday afternoon; no driver noticed.

  4. 2023 — 2024

    New driver app

    Flutter app for iOS and Android with offline vehicle checks, photo evidence and digital delivery notes.

  5. 2025 — today

    Releases twice a week, shared roadmap

    A monthly roadmap review with the operations director, releases every Tuesday and Thursday, and an on-call rotation covering the 05:00 planning peak.

Inside the product

Dense screens for people who use them all day. Every status a planner can act on is one colour and one word; everything else stays quiet.
